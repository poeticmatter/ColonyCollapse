# Colony Collapse: how to play

*The rules as the game plays them now, in plain language. This version replaces the action tiles of the designer's [Rules v7.1](Colony%20Collapse%20-%20Rules%20v7.1%2011-02-26.md) with action points; the board, tiles and scoring are the same.*

Colony Collapse is a game for 2 to 5 players. You are factions in a bee colony settling a new planet. Over five rounds, new hex tiles join the colony's six districts, and you rally support to control them. At the end of each round, whoever controls the most hexes in a district scores it. Most points at the end wins.

## What's on the table

- **The colony board:** a hub in the middle, a ring of 6 printed hexes around it (one per district), and 5 empty slots in each district, 30 in all.
- **Six issues**, each with its own colour: Clay, Sage, Periwinkle, Stone, Seafoam and Mauve, in that order around the ring.
- **Six issue tracks**, one per issue, running from −2 to +4.
- **30 hex tiles** in a bag. Each tile has an issue on each side; the side face up is the tile's issue.
- **The rondel:** 6 slots, one per district, around a dial that points at one of them. A gold **mark** sits between slot 6 and slot 1.
- **For each player:** a pawn that starts in the hub, and as many control discs as you need.

## Setup

1. Shuffle the tiles in the bag and put 6 in the rondel, each on a random side. The dial points at slot 1.
2. Everyone starts on 0 on every track.
3. A random player takes the first turn.

## Words you'll need

- The **dial tile** is the rondel tile the dial points at.
- To **rally** a hex is to compare yourself with the disc on it, on that hex's issue:
  - **No disc:** you put yours there if you're on 0 or more.
  - **Someone else's disc:** you take it over if you're higher than its owner on that issue.
  - **Your own disc:** nothing happens.
- **Control:** a hex is controlled by whoever's disc is on it. A pawn standing on a hex also gives its owner control of it while it stays there, disc or not.

## Your turn

Players take turns clockwise. On your turn you have **5 action points**. Spend them on any of these, in any order, as often as you like:

| Cost | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Turn the dial** one more step. |
| 1 | **Move your pawn** to a hex next to it. |
| 1 | **Rally the dial tile.** |
| 1 | **Address the dial tile:** go up 1 on its issue, then flip it. |
| 1 | **Shift:** go up 1 on one track and down 1 on a neighbouring track. |
| 3 | **Rally around your pawn:** the hex it stands on and every hex next to it. Not while it's in the hub. |

- **Pawns** can't enter a hex with another pawn on it, and once a pawn has left the hub it can never go back.
- **Shift's neighbours** follow the issue order; Clay and Mauve are not neighbours. You can't go down on a track that's at −2, or up on one that's at +4.
- You can't pay for a rally that would change nothing.

Your turn ends when your points are spent, or earlier if you choose; points you don't spend are lost. Then **the dial turns one step**.

## The mark ends the round

When the dial passes the mark, from slot 6 to slot 1, the round ends at once and polling follows. That usually happens when a turn ends, so a round lasts at most 6 turns in all, shared around the table. If you pay to turn the dial past the mark, your turn ends right there and your other points are lost.

The next player clockwise starts the new round, with the dial back on slot 1.

## Polling

When the round ends:

1. **Tiles join the board.** Each rondel tile goes to its district, on the slot for this round. If that slot is taken, it goes to the next free slot, and if the district is full, to the next district clockwise.
2. **New tiles without a disc** go to the leader of their issue: the one player highest on that track, on 0 or more. If the lead is tied, or nobody is on 0 or more, the tile stays uncontrolled.
3. **Each district scores:** whoever controls the most hexes there (its tiles and its printed hex) scores **1 point in round 1, 2 in round 2, and so on up to 5**. A tie goes to whichever tied player controls the printed hex; if none of them does, nobody scores.
4. Six new tiles fill the rondel.

## End of the game

The game ends after round 5's polling. Most points wins. If points tie, the player with the most discs on the board wins; if that ties too, the player earliest in clockwise order from whoever started round 5.
